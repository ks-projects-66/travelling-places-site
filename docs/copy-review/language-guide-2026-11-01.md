# Travelling Places: website copy review and language guide

Supplied by Karim on 1 November 2026. Reproduced as given.

The opportunity is to sound like the travel advisor people already know and trust on Tamborine Mountain: warm, well travelled, quietly knowledgeable and delighted to help. The website can bring that voice together with the polish and sense of occasion found on Belmond and Abercrombie & Kent.

The strongest positioning is:
Personal travel planning from Tamborine Mountain, with the experience and connections to take you somewhere extraordinary.

That line is a proposed direction, not a finished tagline. Its value is the balance: Travelling Places is a real local business, with real people clients can visit, and it also arranges ambitious, special travel around the world.

(Karim, 1 November 2026: say "relationships", never "connections".)

Review scope: I reviewed the visible Travelling Places site on 1 November 2026, including the home page, Tailor-made, Luxury, About, Journal, the Antarctica article, Contact and Privacy. I also reviewed public material about Gina and Travelling Places, plus Belmond and Abercrombie & Kent. I could not find Gina's attachments in the workspace, so I have not compared the draft against her original files. The supplied Virtuoso page displayed Travelling Places branding and links to hotels, cruises, tours and destinations, but its detailed collection did not load; I have therefore not treated individual listings as evidence of trips TP has sold.

## 1. What the research reveals

### Travelling Places already has a distinctive story

Gina's own words are more distinctive than much of the current website copy. In a local interview, she describes getting to know clients, finding out what inspires a particular trip, sharing the pros and cons of destinations, and hearing how people's travels went when they return. She also explains that some clients book a package while others need help assembling a more involved journey. That is a generous, approachable view of travel planning, not one limited to a particular price point. (Escape to Tamborine Mountain interview)

Her more recent local writing combines specific travel knowledge with an invitation to call or visit. A piece on small-group travel, for example, talks about boutique hotels, local encounters and the pace of a trip. A separate article discusses Viking and G Adventures using the team's experiences as a starting point. These are useful models for website copy: name the experience, explain who it might suit, and bring the conversation back to a person at TP.

The voice I would protect is Gina talking to an interested client across the desk: pleased by the possibilities, honest about the choices, and never trying too hard to impress.

### What to learn from the industry leaders

| | What the brand does well | What Travelling Places should take from it |
|---|---|---|
| Belmond | Makes luxury tangible through place, atmosphere and particular details: historic carriages, dining, craft, scenery and people. Its editorial stories give readers something interesting to learn. | Use a specific image or detail to make a trip desirable. Let the reader imagine being there. Keep TP's language less theatrical and more conversational. |
| Abercrombie & Kent | Leads with the emotional significance of travel, then supports its premium positioning with the scale and experience of an established travel business. | Give important trips emotional weight, while explaining the advice and arrangements that make them possible. Avoid relying on grand claims alone. |
| Travelling Places | Has a founder, a local home, long client relationships, practical judgement and access to travel partners. | Put those human advantages at the centre. TP's client should feel they can ask a straightforward question and receive a thoughtful answer. |

The distinction matters: Belmond often describes experiences it operates; A&K describes travel it designs and delivers. TP's opportunity is to be the trusted advisor who understands those possibilities, knows the client, and helps select and connect the right elements. The site should not imply TP owns, operates or has personally experienced a product unless that is true.

## 2. The main copywriting issues

1. **The site says "personal" more often than it demonstrates it.** "Thoughtful", "personal", "tailor-made" and "every detail" recur throughout the site. Repetition makes them less persuasive. Show what personal service looks like: asking whether someone enjoys early starts; explaining the differences between two ships; remembering a preference for a smaller hotel; allowing time between flights; or calling a client when plans change. Use real examples supplied by the team.
2. **The best local advantage is too far down the page.** The home page could belong to many premium travel agencies until the reader reaches Gina and Tamborine Mountain. Bring the office, the team and the long relationships forward. The Mountain is a point of character and trust, not a limitation.
3. **Some copy is more grand than credible.** Phrases such as "knows your destination better than anyone else", "never-before-seen tour" and "organise it flawlessly" overpromise.
4. **The site alternates between three voices:** polished editorial copy, generic luxury copy and longer, more traditional agency copy. The goal is one consistent voice: warm conversation, well edited; knowledgeable without showing off.
5. **Important claims and unfinished items need attention before launch.** "Full financial security: ATIA protected" is especially important to remove or correct. ATIA explicitly states that its accreditation is not a guarantee of solvency, insurance or compensation.
